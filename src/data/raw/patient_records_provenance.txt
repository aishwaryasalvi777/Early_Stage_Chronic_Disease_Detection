───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
DATA SOURCES & CALIBRATION
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
This dataset is fully synthetic. Parameters were calibrated to the following
publicly available sources:

Demographics:
  - US Census Bureau 2023 American Community Survey
    Age/sex/race/ethnicity distributions for US adult population

Condition prevalence:
  - CDC National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS) 2022
    Diabetes: 11.6% | Hypertension: 47% | Heart Disease: 6.7%
  - American Cancer Society Cancer Facts & Figures 2023
    Cancer prevalence by type and age group
  - CDC Mental Health Surveillance 2023
    Depression: 8.7% | Anxiety disorders: 19.1%
  - National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH) 2023
    Bipolar disorder: 2.8% | Schizophrenia: 1.1%
  - American Diabetes Association Standards of Care 2023
    HbA1c distributions by diabetes status
  - American Heart Association Heart Disease and Stroke Statistics 2023
    Cholesterol, blood pressure reference ranges

Lab values:
  - LabCorp / Quest Diagnostics reference ranges (publicly documented)
    HbA1c, LDL, creatinine, eGFR, TSH normal/abnormal distributions
  - KDIGO Clinical Practice Guidelines — eGFR staging for CKD

Medications:
  - CMS Medicare Part D Drug Spending Dashboard 2022
    Most prescribed drug classes and approximate prevalence
  - FDA Orange Book — generic/brand name mapping

Comorbidity patterns:
  - Elixhauser Comorbidity Index (van Walraven et al. 2009)
    Correlation structure between conditions
  - Charlson Comorbidity Index weights

───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
GENERATION METHODOLOGY
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. Patient demographics drawn from age/sex-stratified US population distributions
2. Condition assignment uses age/sex/BMI-adjusted prevalence probabilities
   (e.g. diabetes probability increases with age and BMI)
3. Comorbidities assigned with realistic co-occurrence correlations
   (e.g. hypertension and diabetes co-occur at ~2× the rate expected by chance)
4. Lab values drawn from condition-specific distributions calibrated to
   published clinical reference ranges
5. Medications assigned based on condition-appropriate drug classes with
   realistic polypharmacy patterns
6. Encounter history generated with frequency proportional to condition severity
7. All random processes use fixed seed: numpy.random.default_rng(42)

FILE 2: diagnoses.csv (~350,000 rows — multiple per patient)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
patient_id            Links to patients.csv

diagnosis_type        primary / secondary / chronic / acute
is_active             Binary: 1 if condition is currently active

FILE 3: lab_results.csv (~800,000 rows — multiple per patient)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
